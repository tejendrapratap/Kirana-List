# Common Indian grocery list

A starter list of what Indian homes buy, graded by how widely each item is used. It is the source for the app's starter list and its Hindi/English name matching.

**21 core, 48 common and 21 regional items** (90 in all). Units use the app's set (kg, g, L, ml, pcs, dozen, pack); for greens, coriander and curry leaves, pcs means one bunch.

- **core:** about 80% or more of both rural and urban homes nationally, and at least about half of rural homes in every large state. This is the closest honest stand-in for "found in 90% of homes".
- **common:** used by many homes across many regions, or a staple in one season.
- **regional:** concentrated in some regions, or used by a minority of (often urban) homes.

## Core items at a glance

- **Grains and flours:** Rice (Chawal), Wheat flour (Atta)
- **Dals and pulses:** Dal (your household's usual dal) (Dal)
- **Oils and ghee:** Cooking oil (your usual: refined / mustard / groundnut / coconut) (Tel (khane ka tel))
- **Salt, sugar and jaggery:** Salt (Namak), Sugar (Cheeni)
- **Tea and coffee:** Tea leaves / dust (Chai patti)
- **Spices and masalas:** Turmeric (Haldi), Red chilli (powder or whole) (Lal mirch), Cumin (Jeera)
- **Everyday vegetables:** Onion (Pyaaz), Potato (Aloo), Green chilli (Hari mirch), Garlic (Lahsun)
- **Household and cleaning:** Detergent (powder or bar) (Kapde dhone ka sabun / powder), Matchbox (Machis)
- **Personal care:** Bath soap (Nahane ka sabun), Toothpaste (Toothpaste / Manjan), Toothbrush (Toothbrush), Hair oil (Baalon ka tel / Sir ka tel), Shampoo (Shampoo)

## How this list was built

Goal: a starter list of what most Indian homes use, with each item graded core, common or regional. No source gives a per-item "90% of homes" figure. Only about a dozen grocery lines are above 90% in both rural and urban India: any cereal, any dal, any cooking oil, salt, onion, bath soap, detergent, matches, and hair oil/shampoo. Turmeric, potato and toothpaste come close. The core grade is my best stand-in for the "90% list". There are 90 items in total.


### DATA

1) NSS Report 558 (2011-12). This is still the latest published table of the share of homes reporting each item. It counts what a home consumed from any source (bought, home-grown, ration shop (PDS), gifts), not what it bought. The window is 30 days for cereals, pulses, milk, salt, sugar, toiletries and household goods, and 7 days for oil, eggs, meat and fish, vegetables, fruit, spices, tea and snacks. All-India figures come from Statements 3R/3U and 4R-b/4U-b and Tables T4, T9 and T19. State figures come from Appendix Tables 1R/1U/2R/2U. A script read them from the PDF text, and I checked a sample against the pages; a few could still be misread. In the notes, "R / U" means the percent of rural / urban homes.
2) HCES 2023-24 (NSS Report 592). I checked the report itself. It publishes amounts per person and spending shares, but no tables of the share of homes using each item. I used it only for rice/wheat/millet shares (Tables 3.8R/U) and food-spending shares (Tables 3.9R/U). Those home shares could be worked out from the microdata on microdata.gov.in, but I did not do that. I did not check HCES 2022-23.
3) NFHS-5 (2019-21), Tables 10.25 and 10.27.1. These show how often women and men aged 15-49 eat each food group. They describe people, not homes. The same report gives the iodised-salt and sanitary-napkin figures.
4) Pew 2021 (vegetarians, Jains, avoiding root vegetables), Jyothy Labs 2021, and Kantar figures as reported in the press. Kantar's figures are for brands, so they only show the least a whole category can reach.


### GRADES

- core: about 80% or more of both rural and urban homes nationally, and at least about half of rural homes in every large state. Gaps in the small North-East hill states are noted but do not rule an item out. Wheat flour is the weakest core item, because the combined ration-shop + market share is not published.
- common: about 25% or more of rural or urban homes, used across many regions, or a staple in one season.
- regional: either concentrated in some regions (most homes there, few elsewhere) or used by a minority of homes, often urban or better-off. This also covers occasional items.
Items that have no survey line of their own are graded by judgment, and their notes say so.


### LIMITS

- The household shares are 13+ years old. Packaged foods (noodles, bread, curd, paneer), sanitary pads, cleaners and gas lighters have almost certainly grown since then, so the regional grades for urban-leaning items are probably on the low side.
- The 7-day window misses much of the use of seasonal items (mango, peas, carrot).
- Curd, ghee, pickle and sweets made at home are counted under milk or their ingredients, so bought curd, ghee and pickle look rare in the data.
- Brand reach is not the same as category reach. I could not open the Business Standard article (blocked, HTTP 403) and saw its figures only in search summaries.
- Left out on purpose: tobacco/pan, alcohol, LPG cylinders and drinking-water cans.
- Units are from the app's set (kg, g, L, ml, pcs, dozen, pack). For greens, pcs means one bunch (gaddi).
